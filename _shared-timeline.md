# PUSHPAK Grand Challenge 2026, shared details

All three challenges in this track share one timeline, one inbox and one funding ladder. The per-challenge briefs cover only what differs.

## What PUSHPAK is

PUSHPAK is a national mission on drone technology funded by the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology, with INR 82.7 crore in grant-in-aid, led by IIT Bombay. It targets indigenous drone hardware including hybrid drones and Indian processors, plus unmanned aerial systems for disaster management, coastal monitoring and specialised uses. The mission web portal was unveiled at IIT Bombay by the Minister of State for Electronics and IT.

The Grand Challenge is where the mission recruits technical concepts. Techfest is where the finale happens, not what this is.

This is a staged programme, not a hackathon. It runs about 4 months, with evaluation, mentoring and deployment-oriented development at each gate, and winners continue into a Prototype Development Support Programme from December 2026.

## Shared rules

- Register on techfest.org first, under Competitions then PUSHPAK Grand Challenge.
- Every submission at every stage goes by email to **pushpak_gc2026@aero.iitb.ac.in**. There are no forms, portals or upload links anywhere in this track.
- Teams cap at 5 members.
- Project staff, research staff, consultants and interns directly engaged with the PUSHPAK Project, the Drone Centre, or the organising and host institutions are not eligible, whether entering alone or on a team. A team carrying an ineligible member can be disqualified at any stage.
- Participants keep their intellectual property unless agreed otherwise under the Grand Challenge terms. Submissions must not infringe third party IP.
- Unless stated otherwise, teams cover their own development, testing, travel and accommodation costs.
- Proposed solutions must comply with Indian law, aviation requirements and safety protocols. Permissions for testing or demonstration are the team's responsibility.
- The evaluation committee's decision is final. No correspondence on evaluation or ranking.
- Organisers may modify, postpone or cancel any stage with notice through official channels.

## Funding ladder

| Stage | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Stage 1 | Up to 15 teams qualify, INR 1 lakh each |
| Stage 2 | Top 10 reach the finale with domestic travel and accommodation support per IIT Bombay guidelines |
| Stage 3 | Up to 5 winning teams, INR 1.5 lakh to 3 lakh, plus the Prototype Development Support Programme |

Headline figure on the site is "Upto INR 25,50,000" per challenge, which is the ceiling across the whole staged programme rather than a single prize.

Disbursement process is to be communicated later. Check the site and your registered email.

**Open contradiction.** On the Security of Drones page, the structure section says Stage 1 qualifiers receive the INR 1 lakh, while the FAQ says Stage 1 carries no funding and the money arrives with Stage 2. Worth asking before planning around it.

## Who this is really for

The eligibility lists name academic institutions, research labs, startups, MSMEs, defence organisations and industry consortia before they get to students. A student team without faculty backing is at a disadvantage in two of the three challenges. UAV-X is the exception, being simulation-only.
